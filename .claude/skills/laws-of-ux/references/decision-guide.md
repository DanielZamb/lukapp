# Decision guide

Pick principles for the user's actual task. These are prompts for investigation, not mandatory changes. Follow each link for the source's takeaways and examples.

## Choosing and learning

- [Hick's Law](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/): Are choices numerous or hard to compare at the moment of decision? Group or clarify them without burying needed options.
- [Choice Overload](https://lawsofux.com/choice-overload/): Does the interface demand a choice before the person has enough context?
- [Jakob's Law](https://lawsofux.com/jakobs-law/): Does a familiar task use unfamiliar controls or behavior? Keep useful conventions unless a different pattern earns its learning cost.
- [Cognitive Load](https://lawsofux.com/cognitive-load/): Which information must the person remember, and which can the interface keep visible?
- [Miller's Law](https://lawsofux.com/millers-law/): Can meaningful grouping help someone scan or remember content? Do not impose a seven-item limit.
- [Chunking](https://lawsofux.com/chunking/): Do headings and groups reveal the structure of a dense form, menu, or explanation?
- [Tesler's Law](https://lawsofux.com/teslers-law/): Is unavoidable complexity being pushed onto the user when the system could handle it?

## Finding and acting

- [Fitts's Law](https://lawsofux.com/fittss-law/): Are important targets large, reachable, and spaced well enough for the input device?
- [Law of Proximity](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-proximity/): Does spacing make the right label, control, or result feel related?
- [Law of Common Region](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-common-region/): Would a clear visual boundary make a group or section easier to understand?
- [Von Restorff Effect](https://lawsofux.com/von-restorff-effect/): Does the key action stand out among peers? Do not rely on color alone or emphasize everything.
- [Selective Attention](https://lawsofux.com/selective-attention/): Is task-relevant information likely to be noticed amid competing content?

## Waiting, progress, and completion

- [Doherty Threshold](https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/): Does the interface acknowledge an action promptly and show honest progress for longer work? Do not invent a delay to satisfy a number.
- [Goal-Gradient Effect](https://lawsofux.com/goal-gradient-effect/): Would accurate progress and a clear next step help someone finish a long task?
- [Zeigarnik Effect](https://lawsofux.com/zeigarnik-effect/): If a task is interrupted, can the person see what remains and resume it?
- [Peak-End Rule](https://lawsofux.com/peak-end-rule/): Are stressful moments and the final outcome clear, recoverable, and reassuring?
- [Postel's Law](https://lawsofux.com/postels-law/): Can the interface accept reasonable input variation while stating boundaries and errors clearly? Keep data validation and security requirements intact.

## Checking the result

- [Aesthetic-Usability Effect](https://lawsofux.com/aesthetic-usability-effect/): A polished appearance can improve perceived usability and mask task failures. Check whether people can actually complete the task.
